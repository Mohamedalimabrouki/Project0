# Sound for "Aliasing" (the `audio/` folder)

This folder makes **all the sound of piece 02**: the music, the sound effects and the final mix, for the
3-minute film and for the 45-second vertical Short. Everything is generated by code from equations.
There are no recordings, no samples and nothing is downloaded. You do not need to read Python to use it:
one command rebuilds everything and prints a report of what was measured.

The music is 120 beats per minute, so one bar lasts exactly 2 seconds, and every scene starts on a bar
line. The sound effects are placed at the times that the scene files give them.

---

## 1. In one minute

Run these from `phenomena/02-aliasing/code/`:

```
python3 audio/make_audio.py                 # the 3-minute film  (about 2 to 3 minutes of computing)
python3 audio/make_audio.py --comp short    # the 45-second Short (under a minute)
python3 audio/make_audio.py --verify-only   # only re-measure the files that are already built
python3 audio/make_audio.py --refresh-timeline   # force a fresh timeline first (normally automatic)
```

The script reads `build/main_timeline.json` (or `build/short_timeline.json`). That file is made by
`node render.mjs --comp main --lang en --timeline`. If it is older than the scene files, the script
makes it again by itself, so the sound effects always follow the latest scenes.

| File written in `build/audio/` | What it is |
|---|---|
| `main.wav` | The finished mix of the film. 48 kHz, 24 bit, stereo, exactly 180.0 s, -14 LUFS, true peak under -1 dBTP. |
| `music.wav`, `sfx.wav` | The two halves of the mix on their own. They add up to `main.wav` exactly. Use them if you want to re-balance in an editor. |
| `short.wav`, `short_music.wav`, `short_sfx.wav` | The same three files for the Short (exactly 45.0 s, same loudness and peak targets). |
| `report.json`, `report_short.json` | Every measurement, for the record. The same numbers are printed at the end of each run. |
| `plots/`, `plots_short/` | Pictures of the sound (spectrograms: time across, pitch up, brighter = louder) to look at when you cannot listen. |
| `mix_info_*.json` | Small notes the script keeps between a build and a `--verify-only` check. |

The result is **identical every time** (all noise comes from fixed seeds, and the numerical libraries are
told to use one thread). Running the script twice gives files with the same checksum.

## 2. What is in this folder

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `make_audio.py` | The one command. Reads the timeline, builds the music, the sound effects, the mix and the master, writes the files, runs the checks. |
| `score.py` | **The composition**: which chord plays when, every note of every instrument, and the levels that move through the film. Start here to change the music. |
| `instruments.py` | The instruments: pad, pluck, bell, electric piano, bass, kick, hats, rim click, noise swell, and the echo. |
| `sfx.py` | The 16 sound effects of the scene guide (section 11), one function each. |
| `mixer.py` | Balances the layers, adds echo and room, levels each scene, ducks the music under the effects. |
| `master.py` | The last step: tone, fades, loudness and the peak limiter. |
| `dsp.py` | Small tools shared by the others (filters, noise, reverb, panning). |
| `verify.py` | Measures the finished files and prints the report. |

## 3. The music

### 3.1 The grid and the harmony

Home key: **D minor**, with its relative **F major**. Chords are picked from Dm, Bb, F, C, with Gm and
some colour (major sevenths, ninths, a suspended chord). Tuning is equal temperament, A4 = 440 Hz.
One chord usually lasts one to four bars; the bass plays a new note on every bar line.

| Scene | Starts | Bars | Chords | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Hook | 0 s | 9 | Dm, Bb, F, **Fsus2**, F, Bb, C, Csus4 | A soft pulse starts at 1 s and quickens with the car. Tape sag at 7.4 s. Suspended hush from 11.5 to 13.5 s. Build from 14 s. |
| 2 Title | 18 s | 3 | F, Dm, Bb, C | The build lands on the downbeat (soft kick). The warm F major pad blooms while the `hit` lands at 18.85 s. First statement of the motif. |
| 3 Snapshots | 24 s | 12 | Dm, Bb, F, C, Dm, Bb, C | Calm and curious: pad, sub bass, a steady soft pulse, brushed hats after 36 s. Room for the shutter clicks. |
| 4 The trick | 48 s | 19 | Dm, Bb, F, C, Bb, Dm, **F** (lift), Bb, C, **Fsus2** (freeze), C | Small lift at 70 s (F major, an upward run, bells, a soft groove). At 80 s the pulse freezes on one repeated note. |
| 5 The rule | 86 s | 20 | Fmaj7, Dm9, Bbmaj7, Cadd9, Fmaj7, Dm7, Bbmaj7, C, **Fadd9** (115 s), Dm, Bb, F, C | Brighter chords and a firmer, still soft, rhythm. At **115.0 s** the rule appears: C resolves to F, the motif plays, bass, kick and open hat land. |
| 6 Helicopter, lathe | 126 s | 11 | F, C, Dm, Bb, F, Gm, C | Wider and warmer. An octave-up layer joins the pad, electric piano chords, more groove. |
| 7 Sensors, strobe | 148 s | 11 | Dm, Bb, F, C, Dm, Bb, F | The same warmth with a little more energy; the groove thins out in the last bar. |
| 8 Takeaway | 170 s | 5 | Bb, C, **Dm**, Dm9 | The cadence Bb - C - Dm lands on the tonic at 174 s with the motif. A long tail fades to silence. |

### 3.2 The instruments

* **Pad.** Four detuned saw waves per voice, softened by a low-pass filter whose cut-off follows the story
  (dark and closed at the start of the hook, opening as tension builds, brightest at the resolution).
  The pad is **legato**: each of its four voices glides to the next chord tone in about a tenth of a
  second, so two neighbouring notes (an A and a B flat) never sound together and rub against each other.
* **Pulse (pluck).** A marimba-like pluck: a harmonic series whose upper partials die first. It plays on the
  eighth-note grid, with a dotted-eighth ping-pong echo (0.375 s, three sixteenths, so it stays on the grid).
* **Bells** for the motif and for accents: four harmonic partials, two copies detuned by a few cents.
* **Electric piano** (scenes 5 to 7): two-operator FM with a 1:1 ratio, so every partial is a harmonic.
* **Sub bass.** A sine with a whisper of 2nd and 3rd harmonic (so it is audible on small speakers), plus an
  octave below on the important notes. It is always in the middle of the stereo picture.
* **Percussion, light and sparse.** A soft kick (a sine that falls from 125 Hz to 47 Hz), brushed hats
  (filtered noise with a soft attack), rim clicks. The kick makes the bass duck briefly.
* **Noise swells** for the lifts and for the build into the title.
* **Echo and room.** A synthetic hall (3.6 s decay for the lows, 1.3 s for the highs) and the echo above.

### 3.3 Three ideas worth knowing

1. **Five against eight.** The pulse repeats a cycle of **five notes** (root, third, fifth, octave, fifth):
   one note per spoke of the wheel. The bar holds eight eighth notes, so the pattern slides against the
   bar and only lines up again every five bars, a musical cousin of the wagon-wheel effect. Every single
   note is still exactly on the grid.
2. **The motif is 5 - 1 - 3 - 2 - 1** (five notes, again the spokes): C F A G F in F major at the title and
   at 115 s, A D F E D in D minor at 174 s. The same tune, told from the two related keys. The film
   ends on D minor, so the tune finishes on its own home note.
3. **Moments that follow the picture.** They are written into `score.py` with their times:

| Time | Picture | Music |
|---|---|---|
| 7.4 s | The wheel seems to go backwards (the `glitch`) | All pitched voices sag by 3 semitones over 0.4 s, wobble like a slowing tape, and spring back exactly on the bar line at 8.0 s. |
| 11.4 s to 13.4 s | The wheel freezes (`shimmer`) | Suspended chord (F, G, C): the pulse stops after 11.25 s, the bass drops by 4 dB, the hats leave, the room rings. It is the same chord in scene 4 at 80 s. |
| 14 s to 18 s | Question, then the title | Pulse goes to eighths then sixteenths, kicks, a rim-click roll, a rising noise swell. |
| 18.0 s | Title | Downbeat kick and F major. The `hit` (18.85 s) lands while the pad is still blooming. |
| 70 s | The backwards reveal (real speed) | Upward run of five notes, bells, swell, the groove comes in for four bars. |
| 80 s to 84 s | The freeze again | Fsus2 and one repeated note (every picture identical). |
| 115.0 s | The rule appears | C to F, the motif in bells, kick, bass F, open hat. |
| 174 s to 176 s | The takeaway | Cadence to D minor; the last note (D5) rings out. |

## 4. The sound effects

A cue is `{ t, sfx, dur?, gain?, pan?, rate? }`. `t` is a time in the film (the timeline file already
turns scene times into film times), `gain` is in dB, `pan` runs from -1 (left) to +1 (right) and follows
a constant-power law. `dur` is respected: the sound starts at `t` and ends at `t + dur`. An unknown name is
**ignored with a warning**; a cue outside the film is ignored with a warning.

Pitched effects (pop, blip, glitch, hit, shimmer, riser, motor) take their notes from the chord playing at
that moment, so they can never clash with the music.

| Name | Sound | Made from |
|---|---|---|
| `shutter` | A picture is taken | Two short filtered noise clicks 40 ms apart, a tiny mechanical resonance, a soft mirror thump |
| `tick` | Soft clock tick | Short damped tones; alternate ticks are a little higher and lower (tick, tock) |
| `pop` | Soft interface pop | A sine "bloop" that rises into a chord tone, plus a tiny click |
| `blip` | Tiny high blip (sample dot) | A chord tone two to three octaves up, 10 ms decay |
| `click` | Small switch click | A two-stage noise click with a short plastic body |
| `whoosh` | Air movement | Pink noise through a band-pass that glides upwards, drifting left to right |
| `swoosh_rev` | Reversed whoosh | The same idea reversed: it swells and **lands exactly at `t + dur`** |
| `riser` | Rising tension | A noise band and a detuned tone that climb together, ending on a chord tone at `t + dur` |
| `hit` | Deep soft impact | A sine that drops onto the chord root, soft low noise, a short dark tail |
| `shimmer` | Glassy shimmer | Detuned high sines on chord tones with a slow swell, slow decay and random twinkle |
| `glitch` | Digital stutter (150 ms) | A 25 ms grain repeated six times, stepped in pitch, bit-crushed (a small deliberate act of aliasing) |
| `whirr` | Spinning machine (`rate`, turns per second) | Noise plus harmonics of the rotation rate. A wheel with five spokes repeats five times per turn, so the tone is a comb spaced 5 x `rate` apart. It has no single pitch to clash with the music. |
| `car` | Car driving | Engine orders whose pitch follows the hook's speed ramp, plus tyre and road noise (see below) |
| `rotor` | Helicopter chop (`rate`, blade passes per second) | A train of broad thumps and noisy slaps at exactly `rate`; everything under 100 Hz is left to the music |
| `hum` | Lamp hum | 100 Hz and its harmonics, very quiet |
| `motor` | Electric motor whine | A tone that spins up to a chord tone, harmonics, a faint buzz |

**The car.** The wheel-rate keys of the hook (0.35 turns per second at 0 s, 2.0 at 4.5 s, 4.7 at 6.8 s,
5.86 at 10.2 s, 6.0 from 11.4 to 13.4 s, 6.25 at 16.8 s) are turned into an engine firing frequency
`27.6 + 24.5 x rate` in Hz, using the same smooth interpolation as the scene. At the frozen moment (6.0
turns per second) that is exactly **F3 = 174.6 Hz**, the root of the suspended chord: the frozen wheel
gives a steady, in-tune engine note. The Short has its own ramp (its speed keys come 1 to 2 s earlier), chosen by the scene name.

## 5. The mix and the master

* **Layer balance is measured, not guessed.** Each instrument is rendered alone, its loudness is measured
  (the same K-weighting as LUFS) and it is trimmed so that, compared with the pad, the bass is 6 LU lower,
  the pluck 7.5 LU, the kick 15 LU, the hats 17 LU and so on (`LAYER_TARGET` in `mixer.py`).
* **The music bed sits low under the text-heavy parts.** A loudness contour (`CONTOUR` in `mixer.py`)
  levels the music segment by segment. Scenes 3 and 4 are the reference "bed"; the hook build, the title,
  the resolution and scenes 6 and 7 rise 2 to 3 LU above it.
* **Ducking.** Every cue makes the music duck, weighted by how much attention it draws (a hit 0.85, a tick
  0.2, a long car bed 0.12; the weights add up when cues crowd together). The deepest duck is 3 dB, attack
  30 ms early, release 350 ms; it is applied above 150 Hz (the bass moves only a third as much).
* **Stereo.** The pad is wide, the pulse and hats sit a little left and right, the reverb is wide, and the
  whole music is then narrowed (side signal -3 dB). Everything below 150 Hz is kept in the middle, so bass
  and kick are mono.
* **Tone.** A little less low-mid mud (-2 dB at 300 Hz), a broad lift of the presence (+3.5 dB at 3 kHz),
  nothing above about 15 kHz, and a -1.5 dB shelf above 7 kHz on the master so the top is never harsh.
* **Master.** Fade-in over 0.35 s (the first sample is exactly zero); fade-out from 3 s to 0.5 s before the
  end, then exact silence for the last 0.5 s. Loudness is set to **-14 LUFS**. A look-ahead limiter
  catches peaks on an 8x oversampled copy and holds a **-1.5 dBTP ceiling**, half a decibel under the
  -1.0 dBTP requirement, because the film is later encoded to AAC, which can add a little. The limiter
  watches the mix and both stems, so all three files stay under the ceiling. It acts a handful of times
  per film, by well under 1 dB.

## 8. Changing things

* **A chord or a pattern:** `score.py`. Each scene has one function (`_hook`, `_title`, `_explain_a`,
  `_explain_b`, `_rule`, `_world_a`, `_world_b`, `_outro`; the Short has `_short_hook` and so on). Chords
  are listed as `(start, end, "Name")`; the chord table `CH` says which notes each name uses.
* **A moment that must follow the picture** (7.4 s sag, 115 s resolution, and so on): change the time in
  that scene function. If a scene moves, keep its start on a multiple of 2 s (the script snaps it and warns).
* **How loud is something:** `mixer.py` (`LAYER_TARGET`, `CONTOUR`, `DUCK_DEPTH_DB`) for the music,
  `sfx.py` (`LEVELS`) for each effect, `master.py` (`TARGET_LUFS`, `CEILING_DB`) for the whole.
* **How something sounds:** `instruments.py` for the music, one function per effect in `sfx.py`.
  The pad's glide time is `glide_time` in `instruments.py` (0.10 s plus 0.02 s per semitone); lower those
  two numbers if you want the chord changes less liquid.
* **A new sound effect:** write a function in `sfx.py` that returns a `Sound`, add it to `REGISTRY`,
  `LEVELS` and `DUCK_WEIGHT`, and the scene files can use the new name at once.
* **The music tag of a scene** (`music: 'explain'` in the scene file) nudges the groove of that scene: a
  tag that differs from the designed mood changes the hats' level by a decibel or so. The arc of the film
  is fixed by the brief, so the tag never changes the harmony. Unknown tags are reported and ignored.

## 9. Decisions made without asking

1. **The tonic is D minor**, and the relative F major is the "answer" (title, resolution at 115 s). The
   closing chord is Dm9, so the film ends where the motif ends, on D.
2. **The pad is legato** (voices glide) rather than cross-fading, to avoid minor-second rubs at chord changes.
3. **Sound effects pick their pitch from the harmony** instead of using fixed notes.
4. **The car's pitch is tuned** so the frozen wheel gives F3, and the rotor and the whirr are built so they
   have no strong low pitch that could beat with the bass.
5. **The peak ceiling is -1.5 dBTP, not -1.0**, to leave room for the AAC encoding step.
6. **Loudness is set on the whole mix** (music and effects together), and the stems carry the same gain.
7. **The scene tags are hints**: they nudge the groove but never override the designed arc.

**Not done:** nobody has listened to this yet. It is built to be right by construction and checked by
measurement, but ears come first. The most useful things to listen for: the tape sag at 7.4 s (is it
subtle enough?), the hush at 11.5 to 13.5 s, the balance of the effects against the music in scenes 3 to 5,
and whether the pad glides at chord changes feel natural.

## 10. Words explained

* **BPM, bar, beat, sixteenth note.** 120 beats per minute: a beat is 0.5 s, a bar (four beats) is 2 s, a sixteenth note is 0.125 s.
* **Sample rate, bit depth.** 48 kHz means 48 000 numbers describe each second; 24 bit is how finely each number is stored.
* **Stem.** One part of a mix saved on its own (here the music, and the sound effects).
* **LUFS.** Loudness as people hear it, averaged over the whole piece (ITU-R BS.1770). Streaming services aim at about -14 LUFS.
* **LU.** One unit of loudness difference, about one decibel.
* **True peak (dBTP).** The highest point of the smooth curve between the samples. It can be a little higher than the highest sample, and it is what encoders react to.
* **Limiter.** A safety that turns the volume down for a moment only when a peak would go over the ceiling.
* **Ducking.** Turning the music down while a sound effect plays so the effect is clear.
* **Seed.** A fixed starting number for the "random" noise, so the noise is the same every time.
* **Cent.** One hundredth of a semitone.
